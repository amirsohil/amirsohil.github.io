Studio Ghibli — Budget vs. Revenue

The Problem

Studio Ghibli is famous for its films, but fame and commercial strength aren't the same thing. Rather than take the studio's financial reputation on faith, this visualisation set out to actually plot its financial arc across four decades (1984-2023). A total of 25 films, budget against box office, film by film.

Twenty-five films is a small enough dataset that every point matters individually rather than disappearing into an aggregate, part of why the interaction design leans so heavily on per-film detail (hover cards, synopsis text, clickable stills) rather than treating this as a pure statistics exercise. In other words, the trend line is not in focus here, and the chart is meant to be explored film by film.

The Approach

A D3.js scatter plot maps every film's production budget against its box office revenue, with a break-even line separating profitable films from unprofitable ones, and colour-coding by director — Miyazaki, Takahata, and others kept visually distinct. Every point is interactive: hovering surfaces budget, revenue, ROI, and a short synopsis; clicking opens production stills.

The Outcome

Miyazaki's films account for nearly all of the highest-budget, highest-revenue outliers on the chart. Studio Ghibli's blockbuster commercial success is disproportionately concentrated in one director's filmography.

At the smaller end, several films make the case that some of the studio's strongest returns came from its more modest productions.
