The Wonderful World of Pokémon

What actually makes a Pokémon strong, and a 6-Pokémon team built on the answer.

The Problem

For the Onyx Data DataDNA Dataset Challenge (July 2022), the brief was to analyse 801 Pokémon to understand what actually shapes competitive strength, and use that to build an optimised team.

The Approach

Type composition split into single-type versus dual-type, then broken down further with a chord diagram separating each type's role as primary versus secondary. Stats were compared across all 801 Pokémon, segmented by Legendary status, to test whether "Legendary" actually means statistically stronger or is just a lore label. these feed directly into an optimisation exercise selecting a 6-Pokémon team that maximises combined stats while minimising type-based vulnerability.

Two Teams

Two different 6-Pokémon teams were constructed based on the analysis: one being the optimised team and the other comprising of personal favourites only.

Arceus, Dialga, Kyogre, Rayquaza (Legendary) mixed with Metagross and Lucario (non-Legendary). This serves as proof that the model rewarded genuine stat and type-coverage fit, not rarity.

Built purely on preference, included deliberately to make the contrast between "objectively strongest" and "personally preferred" explicit. Garchomp, Genesect, Infernape, Gengar, Empoleon, and Tropius.

The Outcome

Flying is the rarest primary type in the dataset (3 of 801 Pokémon lead with it) but the most common secondary type (95).

Water sits at the opposite end of that same analysis, being the most abundant type overall once primary and secondary roles are combined (131 total appearances across the dataset), against Ice's 38, the least abundant. Reading primary and secondary counts together, rather than as one blended total, is what makes both of those findings visible in the first place.

Legendary status held up statistically: the 70 Legendary Pokémon cluster in a distinctly higher Base Stat Total range than the rest of the field.

Flying is the rarest primary type but the most common secondary type, making it a supporting role, but not a rare one.
