The Problem

For the Onyx Data DataDNA Dataset Challenge (February 2024), the brief was to turn 7,299 electronics reviews — spanning 38 brands, 50 products, and seven retail websites — into a coherent picture of how customers actually evaluate electronics.

Seven websites sounds like a naturally balanced sample until the actual split is checked. Best Buy alone supplied 5,943 of the 7,299 total reviews (81.42%), with Amazon a distant second at 1,154 (15.81%) and the remaining five sites — Walmart, eBay, CDW, Home Depot, Petco — splitting under 3% of the total between them. Any conclusion drawn by simply averaging "across seven websites" would, in practice, be reporting on Best Buy's customers with a thin Amazon garnish.

The Approach

Cross-platform volume analysis compares review counts and ratings across all seven sources. Brand and category-level aggregation rolls 346 product categories into a packed-bubble view, sized by review volume, coloured by average rating. A network graph maps brands to the categories they're actually reviewed in, surfacing shared-broad categories versus brand-specific niches.

The Outcome

Best Buy alone supplied 81.42% of all 7,299 reviews, meaning the seven sources can't be treated as equally-weighted samples. Brand concentration tells a similar story: Logitech (23.18%) and Sony (14.34%) together account for over a third of all reviews.

The rating distribution reinforces the "don't average blindly" lesson from a different angle. 84.33% of all reviews land in the "Good" category, but 164 reviews are entirely unrated — and every single one of them came from Amazon, meaning any completeness check on the dataset has to be platform-aware. The recommendation-status data ties it together: ratings in the "Good" bracket correlate strongly with an actual purchase recommendation, while "Bad" ratings carry comparatively few, meaning satisfied reviewers are actively vouching for the product to the next buyer.

Microsoft appearing in 'Pet Cameras' and Sony in 'Training Aids' are unexpected footholds a flat brand list would never surface.
